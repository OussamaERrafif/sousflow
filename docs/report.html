Section 01

The Problem

The Souss-Massa region produces over 65% of Morocco's citrus and olive exports — yet the sector is facing a quiet crisis. Groundwater reserves are depleting, and traditional irrigation practices continue to waste water through systemic inefficiency and the complete absence of digital monitoring.

Olive farmers face a compounding challenge: irrigation failures — pipe leaks, clogged drippers, pump malfunctions — go undetected for days. By the time damage is visible on the trees, yield loss is already irreversible.

Core challenge: Farmers lack the tools to detect irrigation anomalies early, leading to water waste, crop stress, and financial losses that threaten the long-term sustainability of olive farming in the region.

Section 02

The Solution

SoussFlow is a smart irrigation platform combining IoT sensors and machine learning to detect anomalies in real time and deliver intelligent irrigation recommendations — built specifically for the Souss-Massa region.

Field Research & Validation

To ensure SoussFlow addresses real needs, we conducted on-the-ground research in the Souss-Massa region — visiting both supply-side distributors and end-user farmers.

Supply-Side Visit · SCPC Sapel — Irrigation Equipment Wholesaler, Agadir

We visited SCPC Sapel, a leading irrigation equipment distributor in Agadir, to understand the hardware landscape and identify integration opportunities. Full visit documentation on the following page.

  • Most farmers purchase basic drip systems with zero monitoring or automation capabilities.
  • Compatible sensor hardware (soil probes, flow meters, pressure transducers) identified at accessible price points.
  • Growing demand for "smart" add-ons, but no local supplier offers a complete software platform.
  • Clear partnership potential for bundled hardware + SoussFlow software distribution.
Supply Gap Confirmed Hardware Compatible Partnership Potential
Demand-Side Interview · Olive Farmer — Taroudant Province

We spoke with an experienced olive farmer who walked us through his complete irrigation workflow, from daily scheduling to maintenance routines.

  • Irrigation scheduling is entirely manual — based on experience and visual inspection, with no data-driven decisions.
  • Leaks are discovered only when water bills spike or tree damage appears — weeks after the problem begins.
  • Strong interest in WhatsApp alerts — the farmer already coordinates his entire farm team via WhatsApp daily.
  • Maintenance is reactive: drippers cleaned only after visible clogging causes uneven watering across zones.
Manual Processes Late Detection WhatsApp-Ready

"We know something is wrong only when the trees start looking dry — by then it is too late for the season."

— Olive farmer, Taroudant province

Key takeaway: Both supply-side and demand-side research confirm a clear market gap. The hardware exists locally, but there is no affordable integrated platform that brings anomaly detection and smart irrigation together for this market.

Why This Model Works

Low Barrier to Entry

The installation fee covers hardware and setup. The affordable monthly subscription makes the platform accessible to small and medium farms.

Recurring Revenue

Monthly subscriptions create predictable, sustainable revenue that funds continuous improvement of the anomaly detection models.

Scalable Growth

Each new farm installation has a lower marginal cost. The subscription model scales linearly with the growing customer base.

Value-Aligned Pricing

Water savings and early anomaly detection typically offset the subscription cost within the first months of active use.
